Growing up in a small town in rural southern New Hampshire, bikes quickly became the lifeline for us to visit our friends, go to Baboosic Lake in the summers, and get around town. If we threw bikes in our dad's pickup truck, we could skip Church after Sunday School and bike home from the town next door. That was an easy choice for me and my twin. 😉 # All The Bikes! ![purple coaster break bike](../img/bikes-coaster.avif) I learned to ride on this schwinn baby, in the parking lot of our elementary school. (I'd learn to drive my dad's manual transmission pickup there nearly 10 years later). Bike wasn't great, but it had wide stable tires -- and the coaster brake was fun. I'd hand-me-down this bike to my younger sister. ![puch cruiser 10-speed bike](../img/bikes-puch.avif) My dad, brothers and I all had this bike. My dad liked that it made servicing easy at the neighborhood bike shop in someone's house. They were quite dorky, heavy, and the swept-back handlebars were *absolutely* awful. That steering turned what could have been something close a classic 80s 10-speed into an unstable tank. ![supergoose bmx bike in driveway](../img/bikes-1985_supergoose1.jpg) Middle school! Tired of the PUCH bikes, my twin and I learned how to work on bikes in our basement. We'd "frankenstein" bikes together from spare parts and from castoffs at the town dump's metal piles. We'd rebuild hubs, spokes, cranks, head units - everything. Our favorite were singlespeeds. ![supergoose bmx bike in yard](../img/bikes-1985_supergoose2.jpg) The bike cult classic ["RAD"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rad_(film)) came out in the 80s and BMX bikes were all the rage. I swapped out tires, pedals, grips, the seat, brakes, headset, brake lines, and more, to take an otherwise normal-looking silver chromoly "Supergoose" bike, into a bike I loved to look at and ride. I even got red dice custom tire caps that was a huge crowd-pleaser. We setup dirt trails and small jumps around the property and goof off with our little single speeds. ![xxx](../img/bikes-blue-bike.avif) Though I loved the BMX bike, to get further across town, the bigger tires and multi-speed practicality of a drop-bar 80s 10-speed could not be beat! This waa a pretty basic steelie 10-speed. I had it for 20+ years and it eventually moved from the NH house to the Cape Cod vacation house. ![xxx](../img/bikes-blue-bike-cape-avi2.jpg) Here I am in 2004 having fun with my twin on Cape Cod. It's a low-resolution still-frame from an early ELPH camera video. ![xxx](../img/bikes-trek-930.jpg) I spent my last two college sumemrs in Palo Alto, California. My mentor and I bought the same bike in Redwood City. The 1992 Trek 930 "SingleTrack" hardtail / no suspension. The early days of mountain biking -- these were insanely fun. On weekends, we'd ride all over the Bay Area Peninsula. Purisima Creek was one of our favorites. ![xxx](../img/bikes-inspiration.avif) blue carbon fork road bike ~2004 (=> NH) aluminum shimano ultegra components, carbon fork ![xxx](../img/bikes-madone-nbd1.avif) my first all carbon bike! trek Madone white/black carbon road bike Pictures/2008/02/26 Bought early 2008 - just in time for our first AIDS LifeCycle charity one week ride, SF to LA, in June. ![xxx](../img/bikes-madone-nbd2.avif) ![trek carbon road bike](../img/new-bike.jpg) ![xxx](../img/romeo.jpg) ![xxx](../img/tyres.jpg) Trek Boone CX bike [Quite the Adventurous purchase](../2022-03-road-trek--driving-2000-miles-for-a-gravel-bike/) in 2022. We upgraded the gears so it could climb big hills, and crammed in 40mm tires, to effectively turn it from a cyclocross bike into a legit gravel bike. It has an interesting flex "ISO" piece in the seat post and seat tube junction area that gives it a more comfortable ride. I love the paint job that fades from "atomic red" to teal. ![trek MTB](../img/first-week-of-phoenix-bike-05.jpg) trek supercaliber XC My current beloved bike, [bought in 2023](../2023-06-first-week-of-phoenix-bike/) Upgrades: lighter wheels, dropper post Features a single dual-action lever to open or lock both front and rear suspension. Trek SuperCaliber 9.8 XT ultra-light XC (“cross country”) mountain bike. Under 24 pounds, with 100mm and 60mm suspension shocks. ![trek MTB](../img/first-week-of-phoenix-bike-16.jpg) I ride this most of my six-days-a-week regular biking days. We can bike over to the legendary Joaquin Miller Park (and Redwood Park and nearby parks) right from our house.
